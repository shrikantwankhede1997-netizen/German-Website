German Syntax Master

The "Mittelfeld" Code

German sentence structure is highly logical. Once you understand the hierarchy of adverbial phrases (TeKaMoLo) and the rules for Objects, you can build perfect sentences every time.

1. TeKaMoLo (Adverbial Phrases)

The strict order for answering When, Why, How, and Where.

Te • Temporal
When? How long?
  • • heute (today)
  • • am Morgen (in the morning)
  • • immer (always)
Ka • Kausal
Why? Reason?
  • • wegen des Regens (due to rain)
  • • aus Liebe (out of love)
  • • deshalb (therefore)
Mo • Modal
How? With whom?
  • • schnell (quickly)
  • • mit dem Bus (by bus)
  • • gerne (gladly)
Lo • Lokal
Where? To where?
  • • nach Hause (home)
  • • im Park (in the park)
  • • aus Berlin (from Berlin)

Example Sentence

Wir fahren heute wegen des Regens schnell nach Hause.

We are driving home quickly today because of the rain.

2. Object Placement (Akkusativ & Dativ)

Rule of thumb: Pronouns are lighter than nouns and float to the front.

Rule 1: Two Nouns

Dative Noun comes before Accusative Noun.

Der Lehrer gibt dem Schüler ➔ den Stift.

Rule 2: Pronoun + Noun

The Pronoun ALWAYS comes before the Noun (regardless of case).

Ich kaufe ihr ➔ ein Geschenk.
Der Lehrer gibt ihn ➔ dem Schüler.

Rule 3: Two Pronouns

Accusative Pronoun comes before Dative Pronoun. (The rule flips!)

Er gibt es ➔ ihm.

3. The Ultimate Hierarchy: Objects + TeKaMoLo

When you have Pronouns, Nouns, AND TeKaMoLo in the same sentence, follow this master flow from left to right:

1. Pronoun Objects (Akk/Dat) ➔ 2. Dative Noun (Often here) ➔ 3. Temporal ➔ 4. Kausal ➔ 5. Modal ➔ 6. Accusative Noun ➔ 7. Lokal

Example: Pronoun + TeKaMoLo

Ich gebe es ihm heute.

Example: Dative Noun + Temporal + Accusative Noun

Wir haben dem Mann gestern das Auto verkauft.

*Note: Dative nouns can technically go after Temporal, but usually prefer before.

4. What about the Genitiv (Genitive Case)?

Possession, sticky attributes, and special prepositions.

The Genitive case rarely gets its own independent, floating slot in the TeKaMoLo hierarchy. Instead, it usually behaves like glue, attaching directly to other elements in the sentence.

1. Possession (The "Glue" Rule)

When Genitive shows possession, it sticks directly after the noun it modifies. If the noun moves in the sentence, the Genitive phrase moves right along with it.

Ich gebe dem Bruder meiner Frau das Buch.

"meiner Frau" is glued to "dem Bruder".

2. Inside Prepositions

Genitive is often part of a prepositional phrase (like wegen, trotz, während), which typically forms the Kausal or Temporal part of TeKaMoLo.

Wir bleiben wegen des Regens zu Hause.

Here, the Genitive is trapped inside the "Why?" (Kausal).

5. Verbs with Prepositions (Präpositionalobjekte)

Verbs tied to specific prepositions (e.g., warten auf, sprechen mit, freuen auf).

Some verbs require a specific preposition. Because these prepositional objects are so strongly tied to the meaning of the verb itself, they tend to sit at the very back of the sentence's middle field.

1. Placed After TeKaMo

Prepositional Objects generally go after Time, Reason, and Manner (TeKaMo), sitting right before the end of the sentence or right before the Lokal element.

Ich warte heute geduldig auf den Bus.

2. The "No Float" Pronoun Rule

While pure pronouns (es, ihm) jump to the front of the sentence, prepositional pronouns (like auf ihn or da-words like darauf) do NOT float to the front. They stay in the back.

Ich freue mich morgen darauf.

"mich" (reflexive pronoun) floats front, "darauf" (prepositional object) stays back.
